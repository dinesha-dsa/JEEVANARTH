## Research procedure

1. Define search strings before screening.
2. Search scholarly databases and relevant public tools separately.
3. Record inclusion/exclusion criteria.
4. Preserve URLs/DOIs/patent numbers and access dates.
5. Distinguish academic evidence from commercial product features.
6. Compare individual features and combinations.
7. Do not infer novelty merely because an identical product name is absent.
8. If pursuing IP, conduct a dedicated patent/prior-art search with appropriate professional review.

## Potential contribution questions

Rather than asking “Is JEEVANARTH unique?”, investigate:
- Which individual components are already established?
- Which combinations are uncommon?
- Is JLI conceptually distinct from existing financial-wellness indices?
- Is MEC distinct from conventional sensitivity/what-if analysis?
- Does integrating second-career, ageing readiness, and family/social support add measurable decision value?
- Can explainability improve financial-literacy outcomes?

These questions require evidence before conclusions.
